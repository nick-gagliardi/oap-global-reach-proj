## Market context

EMEA customers are among the most regulation-driven in the world. GDPR is table stakes; data residency, [PLACEHOLDER] NIS2, DORA (financial services), and national data-protection regimes shape identity architecture decisions directly. Sessions on compliance, data residency options, and European roadmap commitments are strong draws — often stronger than feature content.

Recruitment-relevant realities:

- **Formal approval processes.** Corporate travel policies, procurement rules, and in some countries works-council norms mean the blocker is usually process, not interest. A champion who says yes still needs a documented business case to clear their own system — provide it (Strategy 7's justification letter).
- **Sustainability scrutiny.** Many European companies have flight-reduction policies or carbon budgets; a long-haul trip to Las Vegas gets questioned on footprint grounds. This objection is sincere — treat it that way. The defensible answer is consolidation: one trip replacing several (roadmap briefing, training, exec meetings), not dismissal of the concern.
- **August is dead.** Large parts of Europe are on holiday through August. Outreach that starts in mid-August reaches empty inboxes and leaves no runway for approvals before September 22-24. Front-load EMEA outreach into late July / very early August or plan for a first-week-of-September sprint with pre-approved fast paths.
- **Market diversity.** UK/Ireland, DACH, France, Nordics, Southern Europe, Middle East, and Africa differ meaningfully in formality, language expectations, and travel logistics. Middle East and Africa customers may have US visa requirements comparable to LATAM lead times — check early.

## Tone guidance

- Generally more formal than US norms, with variation: DACH and France expect structured, precise, well-documented communication (titles matter in DACH); Nordics are informal but direct and allergic to hype; UK/Ireland sit between US and continental styles.
- Understatement beats enthusiasm. "Three sessions directly relevant to your DORA program" outperforms superlatives everywhere in the region.
- English is workable for business across most of EMEA, but localized materials for [PLACEHOLDER] German and French markets are recommended — status TBD.
- Respect data-privacy norms in outreach itself: EMEA contacts notice over-personalized emails that imply data use they didn't expect. Keep personalization to information they've shared with us.
