## Market context

Public sector spans US federal, state and local, education, and international government customers — with one shared, non-negotiable reality: **rules govern everything about how these customers can accept invitations, travel, and spend.** A recruitment motion that works for a commercial account can be an ethics violation for a government one.

What matters to these customers on the content side is genuinely strong: zero trust mandates ([PLACEHOLDER] federal directives and state equivalents), FedRAMP/StateRAMP-authorized services, workforce identity modernization off legacy systems, phishing-resistant MFA requirements, and CIAM for citizen-facing services. Oktane's compliance and public-sector track content is a legitimate draw.

The constraints:

- **Gift and ethics limits.** Most government employees cannot accept paid travel, comped passes, meals above de-minimis thresholds, or anything that looks like a gift from a vendor — and thresholds vary by jurisdiction. Even offering can create a problem for your contact. When in doubt, ask the [PLACEHOLDER] Okta public sector compliance contact before offering anything of value.
- **Procurement optics.** If an agency has an active or upcoming procurement involving us, attendance can raise fairness questions. Flag active-procurement accounts to legal/compliance before any invitation.
- **Budget cycles.** US federal fiscal year ends September 30 — the event lands in year-end territory where some agencies have use-it-or-lose-it training money and others are frozen. State fiscal years mostly end June 30. Ask about their cycle; don't assume.
- **Approval chains.** Conference attendance approvals in government can require weeks and multiple signatures, and some agencies cap conference spending outright. Lead times are long even when everyone wants to go.

## Priorities

- Frame attendance as **training and professional development**, not a vendor event perk: certifications, hands-on labs, [PLACEHOLDER] CPE credit availability TBD. Training framing unlocks training budgets and survives ethics review.
- The agency pays its own way. Full stop, unless compliance explicitly clears an exception. Build every conversation on that assumption.
- Lead content-wise with mandates: zero trust progress, phishing-resistant MFA, FedRAMP roadmap sessions ([PLACEHOLDER] session references TBD).
- Time outreach to their budget cycle — for federal accounts, a September event can be a year-end training-budget fit; raise it early enough for the approval chain, [PLACEHOLDER] ideally 6-8 weeks minimum.
- For accounts in active procurement, get compliance guidance before doing anything.

## Tone guidance

- Professional, precise, and mission-oriented. Talk about capability and mission outcomes, not deals or ROI-for-us. "Advance your zero trust milestones" lands; "accelerate our partnership" doesn't.
- Never pressure. Government contacts who feel pushed toward something their ethics office might question will disengage to protect themselves — and they're right to.
- Put things in writing plainly. Your contact may need to forward your invitation to an ethics or approvals office; write every message as if that office will read it, because it might.
- Titles and formality: use them. Government hierarchies are explicit.
