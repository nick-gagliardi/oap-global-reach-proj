## The concern

Harder than "too expensive" — this says the money doesn't exist at any price. It may be literally true (frozen travel budgets, headcount-only spending, a company in cost-cutting mode), or it may mean "there's no budget line called conferences," which is different and workable. It can also be the polite version of "I don't want to fight for this." International customers hit it more often because their all-in number is bigger and travel freezes hit international trips first. Respect the constraint; then find out which kind it is.

## Response approach

1. **Acknowledge**: "Understood — if the budget genuinely isn't there, I'm not going to pretend a discount fixes that. Can I ask a couple of questions to see if there's another path, and if not, let's set this up properly for next cycle."
2. **Find the other budget lines**: conference spend often qualifies under training/professional development (labs, certifications, [PLACEHOLDER] CPE credits TBD), or under an existing project budget if the sessions directly serve a funded initiative. "No events budget" frequently coexists with unspent training budget.

## Reusable talking points

- "When you say no budget — no events budget, or no budget anywhere? Because the labs and certifications often qualify as training spend, which is usually a different line."
- "You're funding [PLACEHOLDER] the identity project already. Three days that de-risk that project can arguably come from the project, not from an events line."
- "Let me check what I can do on the pass side — if I can get the pass covered through [PLACEHOLDER] our customer program, your cost is travel only. No promises until I confirm."
- "If this year truly can't happen, let's lose gracefully: I'll send a justification letter now so it's sitting in your next planning cycle, not starting from zero next summer."
- "What would need to be true for your leadership to fund this? That tells me exactly what evidence to bring you."

## What NOT to say

- Don't treat "no budget" as a negotiating tactic and respond with pressure — if it's real, pressure reads as not listening; if it's soft, pressure hardens it.
- Don't offer comp passes, sponsorships, or cost-splits you haven't verified exist — a retracted offer is worse than no offer.
- Don't offer anything of value to public sector contacts without compliance clearance — this is an ethics rule, not a sales judgment call.
- Don't disappear after a no. The account that gets a graceful next-cycle plan this year is the easy registration next year.
- Don't say "it's only [PLACEHOLDER] $X,XXX" — minimizing their constraint is the fastest way to end the conversation.
