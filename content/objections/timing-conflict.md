## The concern

September 22-24 lands in a crowded season: quarter-end and fiscal-close crunch for many companies (and US federal fiscal year-end on September 30), budget-planning cycles, internal kickoffs, and — for international attendees — the trip expands the conflict to most of a week once travel days are counted. When a CFO's team says "we close the quarter that week," that is a real constraint, not an excuse. Sometimes, though, "timing conflict" is a soft no from someone who hasn't seen enough value to fight for the dates — you need to figure out which one you're hearing.

## Response approach

1. **Acknowledge**: "That's a genuine conflict — I'm not going to pretend fiscal close moves for a conference. Let's see if there's a version of this that works around it."
2. **Diagnose gently**: ask which days specifically conflict. A close process might consume September 30, not the 22nd-24th; an internal event might overlap one day of three. Precision often shrinks the conflict.
3. **Offer partial attendance**: two days, or even one well-planned day plus travel, can capture the highest-value items if you build the agenda around them — [PLACEHOLDER] key-day guidance TBD once agenda publishes (which day carries the exec meetings, which carries the labs).
4. **Offer a delegate**: if the champion can't leave during close, who on their team can? A senior architect or deputy attending with a clear brief — sessions to cover, questions to ask, the exec meeting to attend — keeps the value without the calendar fight. Pair this with a post-event readout to the champion.
5. **Preserve the relationship if it's truly immovable**: pivot to digital access for this year plus a [PLACEHOLDER] post-Oktane regional briefing or roadmap session, and put next year's dates on their calendar early. A graceful no this year protects an easy yes next year.

## Reusable talking points

- "Which specific days are the problem? If it's the 30th, we're actually fine — the event wraps the 24th."
- "You don't need all three days. Tell me your one immovable internal date and I'll build the best [PLACEHOLDER] two-day agenda around it."
- "If you can't be out of the building that week, who's your best delegate? I'll help you write their brief so the trip reports back to you."
- "If it genuinely can't happen this year, let's do two things: digital access now, and a hold on next year's dates before your planning cycle fills them."
- For PubSec: "Your fiscal year ends the 30th — some agencies treat this as exactly the right week for year-end training spend. Worth asking your budget office before ruling it out."

## What NOT to say

- Don't argue with their calendar ("surely close can spare you for two days") — you're asking them to deprioritize their actual job.
- Don't oversell partial attendance as equal to full attendance; be honest about what a shorter trip trades away, then maximize it.
- Don't treat the delegate option as a consolation prize — sent well, it's a genuinely good outcome, and framing it as second-best undermines the delegate.
- Don't keep pushing after a clearly immovable conflict; convert to next-year motion instead of forcing a this-year no that hardens.
