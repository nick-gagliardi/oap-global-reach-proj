## Market context

LATAM is one of the fastest-growing regions for cloud and identity adoption, driven by digital banking, e-commerce expansion, and modernization programs across Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Chile, and Argentina. Security maturity varies widely: some customers run sophisticated zero trust programs, others are early in moving off on-prem directories. Fraud and account-takeover pressure in fintech and retail is intense, which makes CIAM and adaptive MFA content highly relevant.

Three realities shape conference recruitment:

- **Budget and exchange-rate sensitivity.** Costs are approved in local currency against a US-dollar price. FX volatility means a trip that was affordable at approval time can look different at payment time. Total trip cost is a genuine constraint, not a negotiating posture.
- **Visa lead times.** US visitor-visa appointment wait times from some LATAM cities can be weeks to months depending on consulate. For a September 22-24 event, visa logistics must start immediately for anyone without a valid US visa. This is the single most common silent killer of LATAM attendance.
- **Partner-centric relationships.** Much of the region buys through distributors and resellers who own the day-to-day relationship. Invitations often land better through the partner (see Strategy 4).

- Raise the visa question in the **first** conversation, before budget. An interested customer who can't get an appointment is a lost registration no discount can save. Event is Sept 22–24 at Caesars Forum, Las Vegas.

- Push partner-led delegations — traveling as a group with a partner they trust lowers the barrier dramatically for first-time attendees.
- Emphasize CIAM, fraud prevention, and MFA content for fintech and retail accounts; workforce modernization for enterprises and conglomerates.
- Suggest bundling Oktane with other US-side meetings so airfare amortizes across justifications.

## Tone guidance

- Warm and relationship-first. LATAM business culture values personal rapport; a purely transactional invite underperforms. Reference the relationship and the customer's specific projects.
- Language: Spanish for Spanish-speaking markets, **Portuguese for Brazil** — never send Spanish-language materials to Brazilian contacts. English is workable at exec level but localized materials signal respect. [PLACEHOLDER] Localized invite templates TBD.
- Formality is moderate: professional but personable. First names are generally fine after introduction.
- Follow up by phone or WhatsApp where the relationship supports it — email-only sequences get slower responses in much of the region. Follow your local team's norms on channel choice.
