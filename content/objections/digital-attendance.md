## The concern

This is a rational position, and the customer knows it. Keynotes and many sessions will be recorded or streamed; watching from a desk costs nothing and requires no approvals. For international customers the logic is even stronger — they're weighing recordings against a [PLACEHOLDER] 12-hour flight. Some have also been burned before: attended a conference, sat in sessions all day, and concluded they could have gotten the same content from videos. If our answer is "but the energy!", we lose. The objection is only beatable with a concrete list of things that do not exist online.

## Response approach

Concede the premise before countering it — credibility here is everything:

1. **Acknowledge**: "You're right, and I'd never suggest flying to Las Vegas to watch presentations — the keynotes and many breakouts will be available online, and for pure content the recordings are genuinely fine."
2. **Draw the line**: then walk through what is in-person only, item by item, and tie each item to something this customer specifically wants.
3. **The in-person-only list**:
   - **Executive meetings.** Face time with Okta product and engineering leadership that would take [PLACEHOLDER] a quarter of meeting requests to assemble otherwise. Not streamed, not available later.
   - **NDA roadmap sessions.** Forward-looking roadmap discussed under NDA is precisely the content that never appears in recordings.
   - **Hands-on labs.** Guided labs with engineers in the room to unstick you on the spot. A video of a lab is not a lab.
   - **Peer networking.** The hallway conversation with someone running your architecture at your scale in your industry. This is the item past attendees most often cite as the highest-value part of the event, and it has no digital equivalent.
   - **Timezone reality** (APJ especially): "watching live" means 3 a.m. sessions. Realistically it means recordings, alone, weeks later, with no ability to ask a question.
4. **Offer the hybrid**: propose sending one or two people for the in-person-only items while the broader team uses digital access — this converts a refusal into a smaller yes.

## Reusable talking points

- "Watch the sessions online — seriously. Come for the things that aren't sessions."
- "The recordings can tell you what we announced. The exec meeting is where you influence what we build next."
- "Nobody flies for content anymore. People fly for access: roadmap under NDA, engineers at a lab bench, and peers who've already solved your problem."
- "If the whole team can't travel, send the architect for the labs and NDA sessions, and let everyone else stream. I'll help you pick which [PLACEHOLDER] two days matter most."
- "[PLACEHOLDER] customer example — an attendee last year told us the conversation that changed their deployment plan happened at a table between sessions, not in one."

## What NOT to say

- Don't dispute that content is available online — it is, and pretending otherwise destroys trust immediately.
- Don't lean on atmosphere ("the energy is amazing") as the primary argument; it confirms their suspicion that in-person is entertainment.
- Don't guilt-trip ("everyone else is coming").
- Don't promise NDA roadmap specifics or exec meetings you haven't confirmed can happen for this account — check [PLACEHOLDER] the exec-meeting request process first.
