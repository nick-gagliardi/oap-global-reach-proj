## The concern

Fair point — they invested last year, and if this year looks like a rerun, skipping is the responsible call. This objection often hides a sharper one: "last year didn't obviously pay off," or "the same person going twice is hard to justify." International customers feel it more acutely because the trip cost repeats in full even if the content only half-changes. The objection deserves a real answer about what is different, not a reflexive "every year is better."

## Response approach

1. **Acknowledge**: "That's fair — you made the investment last year, and going back for the same experience wouldn't make sense. Let me tell you what's actually different, and one thing I'd do differently this time."
2. **What's new this year**: be specific, not superlative — [PLACEHOLDER] new product/roadmap themes for 2026 TBD once agenda locks; [PLACEHOLDER] new tracks or formats TBD; [PLACEHOLDER] identity-security landscape changes since last year (new threats, new regulations) that reshape the content. Do not improvise announcements — use only what marketing has cleared.
3. **Different person, different conference**: last year's attendee saw one slice. This year, send a different persona — if the architect went, send the security lead or an admin for certifications; if the CISO went, send the practitioner who has to implement what the CISO decided. The event is genuinely different per role.
4. **Returners go deeper**: repeat attendees skip the orientation curve — advanced labs, [PLACEHOLDER] 300-level sessions, roadmap conversations that build on last year's context, and a peer network they already started. Year two is where the compounding happens.
5. **Ask about last year**: "What was the most valuable thing from last year's trip?" Their answer tells you exactly what to anchor this year's pitch on — and surfaces whether the real objection is unrealized value, which you should address head-on instead of papering over.

## Reusable talking points

- "Going back for the same experience wouldn't make sense — so let's design a different one."
- "Who went last year? Because my honest suggestion might be sending someone else this time."
- "Last year you learned the landscape. This year, with an agenda built in advance — labs booked, exec meeting requested — the trip does double the work."
- "A lot has changed in twelve months: [PLACEHOLDER] cleared 2026 themes TBD. The sessions your team cared about last year have sequels, not reruns."
- "What did your team do with what they brought back last year? Let's make sure this year's trip is built around finishing that."

## What NOT to say

- Don't claim "this year is completely different" without specifics — returners can tell.
- Don't imply last year's trip was the lesser one ("this year is the big one") — it retroactively devalues the investment they already defended internally.
- Don't pressure the same individual to return if their role got what it needed; pushing the wrong persona for a second trip burns the account's goodwill for future years.
