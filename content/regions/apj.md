## Market context

APJ is not one market. Japan, Australia/New Zealand, India, ASEAN, and Korea differ in language, buying culture, security priorities, and travel appetite — a single "APJ pitch" will underperform everywhere. What they share is distance: Las Vegas is a [PLACEHOLDER] 10-17 hour flight and effectively a full week away from the office including recovery, which makes Oktane the most expensive conference decision an APJ customer will make this year in time terms, regardless of price.

Other regional realities:

- **Relationship-driven sales cultures.** In Japan and Korea especially, who extends the invitation matters as much as what is offered. Seniority signals seriousness; a cold invite from an unfamiliar rep can read as presumptuous. Partner intermediation is often the right path.
- **Regional events competition.** Customers can attend [PLACEHOLDER] regional Okta events and major vendor conferences closer to home. The pitch must answer "why Vegas instead of the regional event" explicitly — the answer is exec access, NDA roadmap depth, and labs that regional events don't replicate.
- **Timezone friction.** Live-streamed sessions run in the middle of the APJ night, which actually strengthens the in-person case: "watching online" realistically means watching recordings, alone, weeks later.
- **Growth priorities.** Strong cloud adoption momentum; CIAM for digital-native businesses in ASEAN and India, workforce consolidation and zero trust in ANZ and Japan, and government-adjacent compliance frameworks ([PLACEHOLDER] country-specific schemes TBD) in several markets.

## Priorities

- Justify the distance explicitly: exec meetings, NDA roadmap sessions, and hands-on labs — things with no remote equivalent. Acknowledge the burden; don't minimize it.
- Route invitations through partners or senior Okta leaders where culture expects it (Japan, Korea in particular).
- Ask about delegation options: [PLACEHOLDER] APJ group programming, delegation dinners, or an APJ attendee track — confirm what exists this year before promising.
- Position against regional events honestly — recommend the regional event for personas who only need broad content, and reserve the Oktane push for accounts where the in-person-only value is real. Selective pushes protect credibility.
- Start early. Between travel approvals, visa needs for some countries ([PLACEHOLDER] India, Vietnam, others TBD), and long-haul booking economics, late invitations fail quietly.

## Tone guidance

- Formality varies by market: high in Japan and Korea (family names, honorifics, formal written style); moderate and direct in ANZ; relationship-warm and increasingly direct in India and ASEAN. When unsure, err formal.
- In Japan, written materials matter — a well-prepared, [PLACEHOLDER] Japanese-language one-pager the champion can circulate internally often does more than a call. Decisions are frequently made by consensus after the meeting, not in it.
- Avoid hard-close pressure tactics region-wide; urgency should come from real deadlines (early-bird, visa timelines), stated plainly.
