## The concern

Harder than "too expensive" — this says the money doesn't exist at any price. It may be literally true (frozen travel budgets, headcount-only spending, a company in cost-cutting mode), or it may mean "there's no budget line called conferences," which is different and workable. It can also be the polite version of "I don't want to fight for this." International customers hit it more often because their all-in number is bigger and travel freezes hit international trips first. Respect the constraint; then find out which kind it is.

## Response approach

1. **Acknowledge**: "Understood — if the budget genuinely isn't there, I'm not going to pretend a discount fixes that. Can I ask a couple of questions to see if there's another path, and if not, let's set this up properly for next cycle."
2. **Find the other budget lines**: conference spend often qualifies under training/professional development (labs, certifications) or under an existing project budget if the sessions directly serve a funded initiative. "No events budget" frequently coexists with unspent training budget.
3. **Reduce the ask**: one attendee instead of three; early-bird pricing (cutoff July 29, 2026); cost-splitting — the customer pays travel while the pass comes from a customer entitlement code or Free Ticket Nomination, if your account qualifies. **Verify program availability with your regional lead before mentioning it, and never offer comps to public sector contacts — see the pubsec region page.**
4. **Know the pass programs available this year:**
   - **$200 flat rate (code: OKTN200)** — open to anyone, no approval needed
   - **Free Ticket Nomination** — via SFDC "Oktane Nomination" button; 100% off Standard passes; approval within 3 business days; requires a stated business reason
   - **Account entitlement codes** (Gold/Silver/Premier support tier) — auto-generated per account; found in SFDC on the Account layout under "Show More"; use only codes where Promo Code Name includes OKT26 and IsActive = True
   - **CPQ (passes in deal)** — free passes based on deal ARR: <$200k = 1 free; $200k–$1m = 2 free; $1m–$5m = 4 free; $5m+ = 6 free. Unlimited additional passes discountable up to 50%.
   - **Group pricing** — 10% off for 3–5 full conference passes; 25% off for 6–10 (applicable to going rate; not stackable)
   - **NPO pricing** — 5 free passes per `.org` organization
   - **Public sector** — $200/pass for `.gov`, `.edu`, `.mil` attendees, no limit
5. **If it's genuinely no**: convert to next-cycle motion. Ask when budget planning happens, get Oktane into that cycle with a justification letter, and offer Oktane Digital (free, ungated on okta.com) plus a regional event this year so the relationship keeps momentum.

## Reusable talking points

- "When you say no budget — no events budget, or no budget anywhere? Because the labs and certifications often qualify as training spend, which is usually a different line."
- "You're funding the identity project already. Three days that de-risk that project can arguably come from the project, not from an events line."
- "Let me check what I can do on the pass side — there's a $200 flat-rate option open to everyone, and if your account qualifies for an entitlement code the pass may be free. Your cost would be travel only."
- "If this year truly can't happen, let's lose gracefully: I'll send a justification letter now so it's sitting in your next planning cycle, not starting from zero next summer."
- "What would need to be true for your leadership to fund this? That tells me exactly what evidence to bring you."

## What NOT to say

- Don't treat "no budget" as a negotiating tactic and respond with pressure — if it's real, pressure reads as not listening; if it's soft, pressure hardens it.
- Don't offer comp passes, sponsorships, or cost-splits you haven't verified exist — a retracted offer is worse than no offer.
- Don't offer anything of value to public sector contacts without compliance clearance — this is an ethics rule, not a sales judgment call.
- Don't disappear after a no. The account that gets a graceful next-cycle plan this year is the easy registration next year.
- Don't say "it's only $X,XXX" — minimizing their constraint is the fastest way to end the conversation.
